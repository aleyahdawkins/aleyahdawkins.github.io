2. Your bowler & release

Offset: distance from your sliding foot to the ball at release, toward your throwing hand. Drift: positive means toward higher board numbers. Measure these from your own shots.

How the model uses your inputs

Gender, height, body weight, and ball weight are profile information only; they do not determine stance or hook. Ball diameter controls geometric contact width. Speed controls animation time. For hook mode, a deliberately simple, uncalibrated curve uses rev rate, speed, and oil level. Ball coverstock, axis rotation, lane pattern, and pin collisions are not modeled.

The search only suggests a line when it geometrically contacts every selected pin. If none is found, the planner displays that it is not possible with the current modeled shot options. The search checks release boards in half-board increments and five aim offsets per selected pin; it does not prove that every possible real-world throw is impossible. These are possible contacts, not predicted knocked-down pins. Splits and strikes usually depend on pin action; this model cannot estimate conversion probability. The drawn lane is compressed lengthwise.
