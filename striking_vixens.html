Collision model & calibration

Ball and pins exchange momentum through two-dimensional collision impulses. Ball weight sets ball mass; pins use an assumed mass of 1.6 kg. Moving pins can strike other pins. Sliding friction slows pins; restitution controls bounce. A pin is classified as fallen when its simulated speed exceeds the adjustable toppling threshold. This is a proxy for tipping, not a three-dimensional tipping calculation.

Standing pins use circular footprints; fallen pins use enlarged circular footprints to approximate their reach. The model omits actual pin shapes, spin, vertical motion, kickbacks, and pit rebounds. Defaults are assumptions, not measured bowling coefficients. Adjust collision settings to match observed shots; agreement on one shot is not validation.

Speed and rev rate affect the uncalibrated approach curve; speed also sets impact velocity. Gender, height, and body weight do not determine release mechanics. The search evaluates a limited set of lines and optimizes estimated total pinfall. Failure to find a clearing line does not prove a configuration impossible. The drawn lane is compressed lengthwise.
